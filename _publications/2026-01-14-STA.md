### Overview  

End-to-end autonomous driving (E2E AD) systems directly map sensory inputs to future driving trajectories through spatio-temporal modeling, achieving strong performance in complex scenarios.
Despite this progress, the robustness of such systems against physically realizable adversarial attacks—especially at the trajectory planning level—remains largely unexplored.

This work presents **STA (Spatio-Temporal Attack)**, a physically deployable adversarial framework designed to directly disrupt trajectory planning in E2E AD models. Unlike prior attacks that focus on perception outputs or apply frame-wise digital perturbations, STA explicitly models spatially constrained patch deployment and temporal feature accumulation across consecutive frames.

---

### Key Contributions  

- We identify and formalize **trajectory-level adversarial vulnerability** in end-to-end autonomous
  driving systems, beyond perception-level attacks.  
- We propose a **differentiable Dynamic Rendering module** that simulates spatially fixed adversarial
  patches across temporally consecutive frames, improving physical feasibility and spatio-temporal
  consistency.  
- We introduce **Gradient Path Aggregation Optimization**, which establishes temporal gradient chains
  from the final trajectory output to enhance attack effectiveness against planning modules.  
- Extensive experiments demonstrate the effectiveness and generality of STA across diverse E2E AD
  architectures.

---

### Methods  

STA consists of two key components.  
First, the **Dynamic Rendering module** models the physical deployment of adversarial patches at
specific spatial locations over time, enabling differentiable optimization under camera motion and
temporal dynamics.  
Second, the **Gradient Path Aggregation Optimization** propagates gradients from the final trajectory
prediction through the entire spatio-temporal computation graph, allowing the attack to exploit
cross-frame feature dependencies inherent in E2E AD models.

This trajectory-driven, spatio-temporal optimization paradigm aligns closely with the internal
structure of modern E2E autonomous driving systems.

---

### Results  

We evaluate STA on both perception-based and perception-free E2E autonomous driving models,
including UniAD, under diverse driving scenarios. Experimental results show that STA significantly
degrades trajectory planning accuracy, achieving up to **11.97 meters of trajectory deviation at a
3-second prediction horizon**. Compared with frame-wise and perception-level attacks, STA exhibits
superior effectiveness and robustness, highlighting critical vulnerabilities in current E2E AD
systems.
